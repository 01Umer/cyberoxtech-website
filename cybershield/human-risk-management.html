The Problem

82% of Breaches Involve a Human. Are You Measuring Yours?

Verizon's Data Breach Investigations Report has confirmed it for over a decade: the human element — phishing, credential theft, accidental disclosure, social engineering — is the leading cause of security incidents.

Yet most organisations respond by sending annual security awareness training and calling it done. They have no idea which employees are genuinely high-risk, which departments click phishing emails, or whether training is actually changing behaviour.

Human Risk Management changes the approach entirely. Instead of treating every employee the same, you measure actual risk behaviour, score it, and focus your effort on the people who need it most.

What is Human Risk Management?

Beyond Training Completion — Measuring Actual Behaviour

Traditional security awareness training measures whether staff completed a module. Human Risk Management measures whether their behaviour actually changed. It combines training data, phishing simulation results, and behavioural signals to produce a continuous, individual risk picture.

🎯

Traditional Training

Measures: Did the employee complete the module?

Limitation: Completion does not equal behaviour change.

👥

Human Risk Management

Measures: How does each employee actually behave under simulated and real threat conditions?

Result: Actionable, individual risk scores you can act on.

📉

The Outcome

Targeted interventions reduce risk where it matters. Lower phishing click rates. Higher reporting rates. Measurable improvement over time.

Each employee's risk score is a weighted composite of several factors: phishing simulation click and reporting rates, training completion and assessment scores, historical security incident involvement, and any manual flags raised by managers or security teams. The scoring model can be configured to weight factors according to your organisation's risk appetite.
