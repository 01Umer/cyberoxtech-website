Regulatory Landscape

Qatar's Cybersecurity Regulatory Framework

Qatar has developed a robust national cybersecurity infrastructure driven by its National Cybersecurity Strategy and sector-specific regulatory requirements across banking, government, and telecommunications.

🏛️

NCSA & National Information Assurance (NIA) Policy

Qatar's National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA) owns the National Cybersecurity Strategy and sets the National Information Assurance (NIA) Policy — the baseline security control framework expected of government entities and critical infrastructure operators. We help organizations map their current controls against NIA Policy requirements and close the gaps.

🔏

PDPPL — Data Protection Law

Qatar's Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (PDPPL), Law No. 13 of 2016, governs how organizations collect, process, and secure personal data in Qatar. We conduct PDPPL gap assessments and build the data protection controls, policies, and documentation needed to demonstrate compliance.

🏦

QCB Regulations

The Qatar Central Bank (QCB) has issued detailed cybersecurity regulations for banks, financial institutions, insurance companies, and fintech operators in Qatar. We conduct QCB cybersecurity compliance assessments and implement required controls for regulated entities.

📡

ictQATAR / MOTC Requirements

The Ministry of Transport and Communications (formerly ictQATAR) sets cybersecurity standards for ICT and telecommunications service providers operating in Qatar. We assist licensed operators with compliance against applicable ICT security requirements.

📋

Sector & Smart City Guidelines

Additional cybersecurity guidelines apply to critical sectors including transportation infrastructure, smart city systems, and digital services providers. We assist organizations in these sectors with compliance planning and implementation.

FAQs

Qatar Cybersecurity — Common Questions

What is Qatar's NCSA and what does it require?+
The National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA) is Qatar's national authority for cybersecurity, responsible for the National Cybersecurity Strategy and for setting and overseeing the National Information Assurance (NIA) Policy — Qatar's baseline security control framework for government entities and critical infrastructure operators. Organizations working with government bodies or classified as critical infrastructure are generally expected to align with NIA Policy controls. Cyberox helps organizations map their existing security posture against NIA Policy requirements and build a prioritized remediation plan.
What does Qatar's PDPPL (data protection law) require?+
Qatar's Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (PDPPL), Law No. 13 of 2016, is Qatar's primary data protection legislation. It governs how organizations collect, process, and store the personal data of individuals in Qatar, setting requirements around lawful processing, data subject rights, and data security safeguards. Organizations handling personal data — particularly in banking, healthcare, and technology — should assess their data processing activities against PDPPL requirements alongside any sector-specific obligations from the QCB or NCSA. Cyberox provides PDPPL gap assessments and helps build the data protection controls and documentation needed to demonstrate compliance.
What cybersecurity regulations apply to banks in Qatar?+
Banks and financial institutions in Qatar are supervised by the Qatar Central Bank (QCB), which has issued comprehensive cybersecurity regulations covering governance, risk management, incident response, business continuity, and third-party security. QCB-licensed entities must demonstrate compliance with these requirements. Cyberox conducts QCB cybersecurity compliance assessments, develops gap remediation plans, and helps financial institutions implement required controls aligned with both QCB requirements and ISO 27001.
Is ISO 27001 recognized in Qatar?+
Yes. ISO 27001 is widely recognized and referenced within Qatar's cybersecurity regulatory frameworks. The QCB cybersecurity regulations and Qatar's National Cybersecurity Strategy both reference international standards including ISO 27001 as a benchmark for information security management. Many Qatari government entities and large enterprises require ISO 27001 certification from their technology vendors and service providers as a procurement prerequisite. For organizations serving international clients, ISO 27001 certification demonstrates the security assurance required by global enterprises.
How long does ISO 27001 certification take in Qatar?+
ISO 27001 certification typically takes 12–24 weeks for most Qatari organizations, depending on organizational size, scope complexity, and starting security maturity. Cyberox's structured four-phase methodology — gap assessment, ISMS design and risk treatment, implementation and internal audit, and certification audit support — is designed to achieve first-attempt certification efficiently. We work with internationally accredited certification bodies recognized in Qatar and guide clients through the full certification process.
Do you provide Arabic-language cybersecurity services in Qatar?+
Yes. Cyberox delivers bilingual consulting and security awareness training. Our CyberShield platform supports Arabic-language training modules, and we can deliver workshops, presentations, and awareness sessions in Arabic for Qatari organizations. Policy documentation and gap assessment reports are delivered in English as the primary language for technical accuracy, with executive summaries available in Arabic on request. Our consultants understand the cultural context of security implementation in Qatar's public and private sector organizations.
