The Banking Threat Landscape

Why Financial Institutions Are Prime Targets

Banks and financial institutions hold the most valuable target data — customer financial records, transaction histories, payment credentials, and critical payment infrastructure. They are consistently among the most targeted organizations by sophisticated threat actors.

The key threats facing your institution include ransomware attacks targeting core banking systems and customer data, phishing and social engineering campaigns targeting employees with privileged system access, supply chain attacks through third-party software and service providers, insider threats from employees with access to sensitive financial data, and Business Email Compromise (BEC) fraud targeting wire transfer authorization processes.

Regulatory frameworks in every market where financial institutions operate have responded with mandatory cybersecurity requirements. Meeting these requirements while maintaining operational efficiency requires a structured, expert-led approach.

FAQs

Banking Cybersecurity — Common Questions

What cybersecurity frameworks apply to banks in the GCC?+
Banks in the GCC face multiple overlapping frameworks. In Saudi Arabia, banks must comply with the SAMA Cyber Security Framework — a mandatory self-assessment framework covering governance, risk management, operations, and third-party security. In the UAE, the CBUAE issues cybersecurity requirements for licensed financial institutions. In Qatar, QCB-supervised banks must comply with QCB cybersecurity regulations. In Singapore, MAS TRM Guidelines apply to all MAS-regulated entities. In Pakistan, SBP has issued a Cybersecurity Framework for banks. Most GCC banks also pursue ISO 27001 certification as a means of demonstrating internationally-recognized security management to regulators, clients, and counterparties.
Is ISO 27001 sufficient for bank regulatory compliance?+
ISO 27001 is a strong foundation but not a complete substitute for sector-specific regulatory compliance. Central bank frameworks like SAMA, CBUAE, QCB, and MAS TRM have specific requirements that go beyond what ISO 27001 mandates. However, ISO 27001 and these frameworks share significant overlap in governance, risk management, access control, incident management, and business continuity. Cyberox designs ISO 27001 programs with integrated cross-mapping to applicable central bank frameworks, so clients achieve ISO 27001 certification while simultaneously meeting the majority of their regulatory obligations — minimizing total compliance effort.
Does Cyberox perform SAMA cybersecurity self-assessment support?+
Yes. SAMA requires annual cybersecurity self-assessments from all supervised entities, with results submitted to SAMA. Cyberox provides SAMA self-assessment support including gap identification, maturity scoring, evidence documentation, and remediation planning for any areas below the required maturity threshold. We help financial institutions prepare robust, well-evidenced self-assessments that accurately reflect their security posture and demonstrate progress on remediation to SAMA supervisors.
How does Cyberox handle PCI DSS requirements for banks?+
PCI DSS applies to any financial institution that stores, processes, or transmits cardholder data. Cyberox assists banks with PCI DSS scoping, gap assessments, and remediation planning. Importantly, we design ISO 27001 implementations with explicit PCI DSS control mapping, so your ISO 27001 Annex A controls directly evidence PCI DSS requirements wherever they overlap. This integrated approach reduces the total audit burden and evidence collection effort when preparing for both assessments. We work with Qualified Security Assessors (QSAs) to ensure full PCI DSS compliance.
