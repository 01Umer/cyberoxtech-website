ISO 27001:2022 — What Changed and What Your ISMS Needs to Do Now

The 2022 revision reorganized Annex A, reduced controls from 114 to 93, and introduced 11 new controls that require fresh evidence, ownership, and risk treatment decisions.

The biggest mistake organizations make is treating the 2022 update as a document renumbering exercise. Auditors expect to see that the organization has reviewed the new control set, updated its Statement of Applicability, and aligned evidence to the new themes.

What changed

Annex A now uses four themes: Organizational, People, Physical, and Technological controls. The new controls cover areas such as threat intelligence, cloud service security, ICT readiness for business continuity, configuration management, data masking, data leakage prevention, monitoring activities, web filtering, secure coding, and information deletion.

What to update

Update your gap assessment, risk register, risk treatment plan, Statement of Applicability, internal audit checklist, policy library, supplier controls, and evidence structure. CyberShield can track the mapping between old controls, new controls, owners, and evidence items.

Recommended next step

Run a focused ISO 27001:2022 transition gap assessment before your next surveillance or recertification audit.
