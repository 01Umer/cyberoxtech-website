ISO 27001:2022 — What Changed and What Your ISMS Needs to Do Now

The 2022 revision reorganized Annex A, reduced controls from 114 to 93, and introduced 11 new controls that require fresh evidence, ownership, and risk treatment decisions.

Get ISO 27001 Support Free Transition Gap Assessment

The biggest mistake organizations make with the 2022 update is treating it as a document renumbering exercise. Auditors expect to see that the organization has actually reviewed the new control set, updated its Statement of Applicability with real justification, and mapped evidence to the new structure — not just relabeled old documents.

What changed structurally

Annex A moved from 14 numbered clauses (A.5 through A.18) in the 2013 version to 4 themes in 2022: Organizational controls (37), People controls (8), Physical controls (14), and Technological controls (34) — 93 controls in total, down from 114. Most of the reduction came from consolidation: multiple related 2013 controls were merged into single, broader 2022 controls, so very little actual control coverage was lost. Genuinely new territory came from 11 brand-new controls addressing gaps the 2013 version didn't cover well.

The 11 new controls

New controlWhat it addresses
Threat intelligenceFormal process for collecting and analyzing information about emerging threats
Cloud service securitySecurity requirements specific to acquiring, using, and exiting cloud services
ICT readiness for business continuityExplicit planning for ICT systems to remain available during disruption
Physical security monitoringContinuous monitoring of premises for unauthorized physical access
Configuration managementDocumented, controlled baseline configurations for hardware, software, and networks
Information deletionFormal, verifiable deletion of information when no longer required
Data maskingTechniques to limit exposure of sensitive data, including in non-production environments
Data leakage preventionTechnical measures to detect and prevent unauthorized data disclosure
Monitoring activitiesSystematic monitoring of networks, systems, and applications for anomalous behavior
Web filteringControls to manage access to external websites and reduce exposure to malicious content
Secure codingSecure development principles applied throughout the software lifecycle

What you actually need to update

  • ✓
    Gap assessment against the new 93-control structure, not just a copy-paste from your 2013 assessment.
  • ✓
    Risk register and risk treatment plan — several new controls (cloud security, DLP, secure coding) likely address risks that weren't formally captured before.
  • ✓
    Statement of Applicability — re-justified against the new numbering, with genuine rationale for each inclusion or exclusion, not a mechanical remap.
  • ✓
    Internal audit checklist and policy library — updated to reference new control names and themes.
  • ✓
    Supplier and evidence structure — particularly for cloud service security and configuration management, where evidence often lives with a third party.

Common transition mistakes

The most frequent finding during 2022 transition audits is a Statement of Applicability that was mechanically remapped from the old control numbers without genuine re-assessment — auditors can tell when justification text was copy-pasted rather than reconsidered. The second most common issue is treating the 11 new controls as automatically "not applicable" without documented rationale, particularly cloud service security and secure coding, which apply to the vast majority of modern organizations.

Recommended next step

Run a focused ISO 27001:2022 transition gap assessment before your next surveillance or recertification audit, rather than discovering gaps during the audit itself. Frequently asked questions

How many controls does ISO 27001:2022 have compared to the 2013 version?+
ISO 27001:2022 reduced Annex A from 114 controls across 14 clauses in the 2013 version to 93 controls organized into 4 themes: Organizational, People, Physical, and Technological. Many controls were merged or consolidated rather than removed; 11 controls are genuinely new.
By when do organizations need to transition to ISO 27001:2022?+
Organizations certified to ISO 27001:2013 needed to transition by the certification body deadline set after the 2022 revision, and new certifications have been issued against the 2022 version since shortly after publication. If you're not yet certified, you should certify directly against ISO 27001:2022.
What are the 11 new controls in ISO 27001:2022?+
The 11 new controls cover threat intelligence, information security for cloud services, ICT readiness for business continuity, physical security monitoring, configuration management, information deletion, data masking, data leakage prevention, monitoring activities, web filtering, and secure coding.
