Methodology

How We Conduct Penetration Tests

Our testing follows recognized international frameworks — OWASP, PTES, and NIST SP 800-115 — ensuring consistent, repeatable, and defensible results.

1

Scoping & Rules of Engagement

Define targets, test type (black/grey/white box), out-of-scope systems, timing, and escalation procedures. Authorize testing in writing.

2

Reconnaissance & Discovery

OSINT, service enumeration, fingerprinting, and attack surface mapping to understand the target environment as an attacker would.

3

Exploitation & Post-Exploitation

Safely exploit confirmed vulnerabilities to demonstrate real impact — privilege escalation, data exfiltration paths, lateral movement.

✓

Reporting & Debrief

Comprehensive written reports plus a live debrief with your technical and executive teams to walk through findings and remediation.

Frameworks We Follow
OWASP Testing Guide (web/mobile), PTES (Penetration Testing Execution Standard), NIST SP 800-115 (Technical Guide to Information Security Testing), OWASP API Security Top 10, MITRE ATT&CK framework for threat simulation mapping.

FAQs

Penetration Testing — Common Questions

How often should we conduct penetration testing?+
At minimum, penetration testing should be conducted annually or after significant infrastructure changes, new application deployments, or major security incidents. PCI DSS requires testing at least annually and after significant changes. For higher-risk environments (financial services, healthcare, critical infrastructure), semi-annual testing is advisable. Many organizations also run continuous vulnerability assessments between annual pen tests to detect new exposures quickly.
What should we expect during a penetration test?+
Before the test begins, we agree on scope, out-of-scope systems, and rules of engagement in writing. During testing, our team operates as an authorized attacker — the process is controlled and safe. We will never intentionally cause data loss, system downtime, or service disruption without explicit approval. We notify you immediately if we discover a critical vulnerability that poses an active risk. After testing, you receive reports and a debrief within the agreed timeframe.
What is the difference between a vulnerability assessment and a penetration test?+
A vulnerability assessment identifies and catalogues potential security weaknesses using automated scanning tools — it tells you what might be exploitable. A penetration test goes further: our testers actively attempt to exploit identified vulnerabilities to confirm which ones are genuinely exploitable and what the actual business impact would be. Pen tests are more manual, more targeted, and produce richer findings. Both are valuable — vulnerability assessments are more frequent; pen tests go deeper.
How much does a penetration test cost?+
Cost depends on scope (number of IP addresses, applications, users), test type (black box vs white box), and depth of assessment. A focused web application pen test starts from a defined fixed fee; large infrastructure engagements are scoped and priced per project. We provide transparent, fixed-price proposals based on a brief scoping call. Contact us to request a quote — there is no obligation and most quotes are turned around within 48 hours.
What credentials or certifications do your pen testers hold?+
Our penetration testers hold industry-recognized certifications including OSCP (Offensive Security Certified Professional), CEH (Certified Ethical Hacker), and GPEN (GIAC Penetration Tester). All testing is conducted under strict confidentiality agreements and rules of engagement that protect your systems and data throughout the engagement.
