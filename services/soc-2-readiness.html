What is SOC 2?

The Trust Standard for SaaS and Service Organizations

SOC 2 (System and Organization Controls 2) is an auditing framework developed by the AICPA that evaluates how service organizations manage customer data based on five Trust Service Criteria.

Enterprise customers, investors, and regulators increasingly require a SOC 2 report as proof that your organization has mature, operating security controls. For SaaS companies, fintechs, and cloud service providers, SOC 2 is often a non-negotiable sales requirement — especially when selling into US, European, or enterprise markets.

Cyberox guides you through both Type I (point-in-time design assessment) and Type II (operating effectiveness over a 6–12 month observation period) engagements.

The Five Trust Service Criteria

  • ✓
    Security (CC) — Protection against unauthorized access (required for all SOC 2)
  • ✓
    Availability — System availability as committed or agreed
  • ✓
    Processing Integrity — System processing is complete, valid, and timely
  • ✓
    Confidentiality — Protection of confidential information
  • ✓
    Privacy — Personal information collected, used, retained in accordance with commitments
Type I vs Type II

Understanding the Two SOC 2 Report Types

Choosing the right report type depends on your timeline, customer requirements, and maturity level.

I

SOC 2 Type I

Evaluates the design and implementation of your controls at a single point in time. Answers the question: "Are the right controls in place?"

  • ✓
    Faster to achieve (typically 8–14 weeks)
  • ✓
    Good for early-stage companies or initial customer requirements
  • ✓
    Serves as a stepping stone to Type II
II

SOC 2 Type II

Evaluates both design and operating effectiveness over an observation period (typically 6–12 months). The gold standard for enterprise sales.

  • ✓
    Most valued by enterprise and regulated-industry customers
  • ✓
    Demonstrates controls operate consistently over time
  • ✓
    Required by many US enterprise procurement teams

FAQs

SOC 2 — Common Questions

How is SOC 2 different from ISO 27001?+
ISO 27001 is an internationally recognized certification issued by an accredited certification body, following a defined standard. SOC 2 is an attestation report issued by a licensed CPA firm, primarily recognized in North American enterprise markets. ISO 27001 certifies your ISMS meets defined requirements; SOC 2 attests to how your controls operate against Trust Service Criteria. Many organizations pursue both. Cyberox can run them in parallel to maximize efficiency.
Do I need a CPA firm to perform the SOC 2 audit?+
Yes. SOC 2 reports must be issued by an independent, licensed CPA firm — consulting firms like Cyberox prepare you for the audit but cannot issue the final report. We help you select an appropriate CPA firm, coordinate the engagement, and manage the auditor relationship throughout the process.
Which Trust Service Criteria should we include?+
Security (Common Criteria) is mandatory for all SOC 2 reports. The additional criteria — Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy — are optional and should be selected based on your service commitments and customer requirements. Most SaaS companies start with Security only, then add Availability and Confidentiality as needed. We help you make this decision during the readiness assessment phase.
How long does it take to achieve SOC 2 Type II?+
SOC 2 Type II requires a minimum observation period — typically 6 to 12 months — during which your controls must be operating effectively. With Cyberox's readiness program, most clients complete control implementation in 8–12 weeks, then enter the observation period. Total time from project start to receiving the Type II report is typically 9–15 months.
Is SOC 2 required for selling to US enterprise customers?+
Increasingly, yes. US enterprise procurement teams, especially in financial services, healthcare, and technology sectors, routinely require SOC 2 Type II reports as part of vendor security assessments. Without a SOC 2 report, deals can be delayed or lost. For Pakistan and GCC-based companies expanding into US markets, achieving SOC 2 is a strategic necessity.
