ISO 27005 Framework

Risk Management Built on the Right Foundation

ISO 27005 is the international standard for information security risk management. It provides a structured, repeatable methodology for identifying and treating information security risks — and forms the backbone of every ISO 27001 implementation.

Many organizations approach cybersecurity risk informally — a list of concerns, a spreadsheet someone maintains sporadically. ISO 27005 transforms risk management into a rigorous, documented process that satisfies auditors, informs leadership, and drives measurable improvement over time.

Cyberox implements ISO 27005-aligned risk management as both a standalone service and an integrated component of ISO 27001 certification engagements.

The ISO 27005 Risk Process

1
Context Establishment
Define scope, risk criteria, and organizational context
2
Risk Identification
Identify assets, threats, vulnerabilities, and potential impacts
3
Risk Analysis & Evaluation
Rate likelihood and impact; calculate risk levels; prioritize
4
Risk Treatment
Select treatment options: mitigate, accept, transfer, or avoid

FAQs

Risk Management — Common Questions

Is a formal risk assessment required for ISO 27001?+
Yes. ISO 27001 Clause 6.1.2 explicitly requires organizations to perform an information security risk assessment using a documented and repeatable process. The risk assessment must identify risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information — and the results must inform control selection (via the Statement of Applicability). Without a formal, documented risk assessment, ISO 27001 certification is not possible.
How often should we review the risk register?+
ISO 27001 requires risk assessments to be performed at planned intervals and whenever significant changes occur. In practice, most organizations review their risk register quarterly, with a full risk assessment annually. Risk register reviews should also be triggered by: significant organizational changes, new system deployments, security incidents, new regulatory requirements, or changes in the threat landscape.
What is the difference between risk assessment and risk management?+
Risk assessment is a specific activity — the process of identifying, analyzing, and evaluating risks at a point in time. Risk management is the broader, ongoing program that includes risk assessment plus treatment planning, monitoring, communication, and continuous improvement. A risk assessment is an input to risk management; risk management is the overall discipline that keeps your organization's risk exposure within acceptable bounds over time.
Can you help us with risk reporting to the board?+
Yes — board-level risk reporting is a core deliverable in our risk management service. We develop executive risk dashboards and risk heat maps that translate technical risk data into clear, visual narratives appropriate for non-technical board members. This directly satisfies ISO 27001 Clause 9.3 (Management Review) and is a key output in any vCISO engagement.
