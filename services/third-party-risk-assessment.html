Why Vendor Risk Matters

Supply Chain Attacks Are Now the Primary Entry Point

Over 60% of data breaches now involve a third party — a vendor, supplier, or service provider with access to your systems or data. Attackers target your weakest link.

Supply chain attacks have become the preferred attack vector for sophisticated threat actors. By compromising a trusted vendor — software supplier, IT service provider, payroll processor — attackers bypass your perimeter defenses and enter through a trusted channel.

ISO 27001:2022 Annex A Control 5.19 explicitly requires organizations to manage information security in supplier relationships. Auditors and enterprise customers increasingly demand evidence of a formal TPRM program.

ISO 27001 Annex A.5.19

"Processes and procedures should be defined and implemented to manage the information security risks associated with the use of supplier's products or services."

— ISO 27001:2022, Annex A Control 5.19

Our TPRM Methodology

A Six-Step Vendor Risk Management Process

1

Vendor Inventory

Build a complete inventory of all third-party vendors, suppliers, and service providers — categorized by the type of access they have (data, systems, facilities).

2

Risk Tiering

Classify vendors into risk tiers (Critical, High, Medium, Low) based on data sensitivity, access levels, business criticality, and contract value. Not every vendor needs the same scrutiny.

3

Security Questionnaire

Send structured security questionnaires (tailored by risk tier) to vendors covering their security controls, certifications, incident response capabilities, and data protection practices.

4

Assessment & Review

Review questionnaire responses, validate certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2), and conduct additional testing or interviews for Critical and High tier vendors.

5

Risk Scoring

Assign a risk score to each vendor based on assessment findings. Produce vendor risk profiles and a consolidated third-party risk register for management reporting.

6

Ongoing Monitoring

Establish annual re-assessment cycles, certification expiry tracking, and incident notification processes. Monitor public threat intelligence for vendor compromise indicators.

FAQs

Third Party Risk — Common Questions

How many vendors should we assess?+
You don't need to assess every vendor at the same depth — that would be impractical for most organizations. The risk tiering approach means Critical and High tier vendors (those with access to sensitive data or critical systems) receive thorough assessments, while Medium and Low tier vendors complete simpler questionnaires or are managed through contractual controls only. Most organizations find that 10–20% of their vendors account for 80%+ of their third-party risk.
What if a vendor refuses to complete a security questionnaire?+
Vendor non-cooperation is a risk indicator in itself. We help you establish contractual requirements (typically in your supplier agreements per Annex A.5.20) that make security questionnaire completion a condition of the relationship. For existing vendors, we develop engagement strategies that frame the questionnaire as a mutual due diligence exercise. Where vendors genuinely cannot respond, we help you assess risk from alternative sources (ISO 27001 certificates, SOC 2 reports, penetration test summaries).
How often should vendor assessments be repeated?+
Critical and High tier vendors should be reassessed annually. Medium tier vendors can be reassessed every 18–24 months. Reassessment should also be triggered by: significant incidents involving the vendor, major changes to their services or infrastructure, expiry of security certifications, or changes in the scope of your relationship. ISO 27001 Annex A.5.22 requires ongoing monitoring of supplier service delivery.
Does TPRM satisfy ISO 27001 Annex A.5.19?+
Yes. A documented TPRM program with a vendor inventory, risk tiering methodology, assessment evidence, and monitoring schedule directly satisfies the requirements of Annex A.5.19 (Information security in supplier relationships), A.5.20 (Addressing information security within supplier agreements), A.5.21 (Managing information security in the ICT supply chain), and A.5.22 (Monitoring, review, and change management of supplier services).
